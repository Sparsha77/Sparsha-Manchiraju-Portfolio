My Portfolio: 
I used to run a news brand. Now I sit in engineering meetings and translate.

Somewhere between a 40-page spec sheet and a buyer who gives you four seconds, there's a story. Finding it is my job.

I started out thinking content was about writing well. Turns out writing is the easy part. Making people care is the hard part.

I founded OrangeNews9 and ran it for four years, then spent a year as a research correspondent at The South India Times. News taught me two things: lead with what matters, and nobody owes you their attention. Then I walked into industrial B2B. Brilliant technology, brochures that put people to sleep. So that became the mission: make complex things feel human without dumbing them down.

These days that looks like:
Positioning that says one thing clearly instead of ten things politely
Campaigns and thought leadership people actually finish reading
Turning engineering conversations into stories buyers remember
Leading a content team that's allergic to the word "synergy"

What I believe: the B2B brands that win won't just build better products. They'll explain why those products matter, in words a human would actually say.

Off the clock, I write personal essays on Substack. The latest one is about café culture, which tells you exactly how I'd like to meet.

If you're building a brand that's smarter than it sounds, let's talk. Bonus points if there's coffee.
